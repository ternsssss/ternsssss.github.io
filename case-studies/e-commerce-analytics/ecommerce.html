1.

Overview

Project Title

E-Commerce Analytics

Business Case

An e-commerce business needs a consolidated view of revenue, customers, subscriptions, promotions, and refunds to understand what is driving growth and where commercial value is being diluted.

Business Objective

Identify the highest-value customer, subscription, channel, product, and pricing opportunities — then translate them into actions that can improve retention, revenue quality, and commercial efficiency.

2.

Business Questions

Business Problem

Commercial stakeholders need a consolidated view of revenue performance, customer loyalty, subscription behavior, channels, products, promotions, and refunds to identify where growth is created and where value is being diluted.

Key Questions

The analysis was designed to answer the following business questions across commercial performance, customer loyalty, subscriptions, and pricing.

  • How is total sales changing over time, and which channels contribute the most revenue?
  • Which customers and channels drive repeat purchasing and loyal customer revenue?
  • Which subscription billing cycles generate the strongest customer value and revenue?
  • How long do customers typically wait before making a second purchase, and where are lifecycle opportunities visible?
  • Which discount codes, products, and channels create the strongest commercial performance, and where do refunds or discount leakage require attention?
3.

Key Insights

1.

Revenue concentration creates both strength and risk

Observation

Annual billing contributes $28.11M, or 88.3% of total net revenue.

Analysis

Annual revenue per customer is $7,050 versus $699 monthly and $1,117 one-time.

Impact

Revenue is highly dependent on annual subscriptions, creating both a strength and concentration risk.

Recommendation

Protect annual retention while developing targeted monthly-to-annual conversion journeys.

2.

The website is the strongest commercial growth engine

Observation

Website revenue is approximately $14.4M, more than twice Direct Sales at $6.4M.

Analysis

The website is also the strongest repeat-customer channel at approximately 3.9K customers.

Impact

Digital self-service is a major acquisition, retention, and monetization engine.

Recommendation

Prioritize website conversion, renewal, personalization, and cross-sell experiences.

3.

Second-purchase timing creates a measurable lifecycle window

Observation

The average time to second purchase is 45.18 days.

Analysis

A large share of second purchases occurs early, while a meaningful customer tail extends beyond 60 days.

Impact

The business has an identifiable period to influence repeat purchasing before customer engagement weakens.

Recommendation

Trigger lifecycle campaigns within the first 20–45 days, with differentiated follow-up for slower repurchasers.

4.

Promotion usage does not automatically translate into loyalty

Observation

WELCOME10 is the most-used discount code at 2.73K users, while overall discount usage is 35.38%.

Analysis

Several major discount codes have repeat-purchase rates around 28%–32%, showing that usage does not automatically translate into stronger loyalty.

Impact

Broad discounting can create commercial cost without proportionate retention value.

Recommendation

Shift from blanket discounts to segmented offers tied to acquisition, retention, upsell, and margin objectives.
