1.

Overview

Project Title

E-Commerce Analytics

Business Case

An e-commerce business needs a consolidated view of revenue, customers, subscriptions, promotions, and refunds to understand what is driving growth and where commercial value is being diluted.

Business Objective

Identify the highest-value customer, subscription, channel, product, and pricing opportunities — then translate them into actions that can improve retention, revenue quality, and commercial efficiency.

2.

Business Questions

Business Problem

Commercial stakeholders need a clearer understanding of revenue performance, customer loyalty, subscription value, channel effectiveness, and promotion efficiency to identify where growth should be accelerated and where commercial value requires protection.

Key Questions

The analysis was designed to answer five management questions across commercial performance, customer loyalty, subscriptions, pricing, and growth.

  • What are the strongest revenue, channel, product, and geographic drivers?
  • How much revenue is associated with repeat customers, and how quickly do they repurchase?
  • Which billing cycle creates the strongest customer economics and revenue quality?
  • Which discount programs are most used, and where could discounting be eroding value?
  • Where should the business prioritize retention, upsell, cross-sell, and premium-market expansion?
3.

Key Insights

1.

Revenue concentration creates both strength and risk

Observation

Annual billing contributes $28.11M, or 88.3% of total net revenue.

Analysis

Annual revenue per customer is $7,050, compared with $699 monthly and $1,117 one-time.

Impact

Revenue is highly dependent on annual subscriptions, creating both a commercial strength and concentration risk.

Recommendation

Protect annual retention while developing targeted monthly-to-annual conversion journeys.

2.

The website is the strongest commercial channel

Observation

Website revenue is approximately $14.4M, more than twice Direct Sales at $6.4M.

Analysis

The website is also the strongest repeat-customer channel at about 3.9K customers.

Impact

Digital self-service is a major acquisition and retention engine.

Recommendation

Prioritize website conversion, renewal, personalization, and cross-sell experiences.

3.

The second purchase has an identifiable intervention window

Observation

Average time to second purchase is 45.18 days.

Analysis

A large share of second purchases occurs within 20 days, but a meaningful tail extends beyond 60 days.

Impact

The business has an identifiable window to influence the second purchase before customers become less engaged.

Recommendation

Trigger lifecycle campaigns within the first 20–45 days, with differentiated follow-up for slower repurchasers.

4.

Discount usage does not automatically create loyalty

Observation

WELCOME10 is the most-used discount code at 2.73K users; discount usage is 35.38%.

Analysis

Several major codes have repeat-purchase rates around 28%–32%, indicating usage does not automatically translate into loyalty.

Impact

Broad discounting can create cost without proportionate retention value.

Recommendation

Shift from blanket discounts to segmented offers tied to acquisition, retention, or upsell objectives.
