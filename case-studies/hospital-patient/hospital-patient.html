1.

Overview

Project Title

Hospital Patient Analysis

Business Case

Hospital leaders need a unified view of patient demand, clinical utilization, and financial burden to identify where resources are concentrated, where costs are rising, and where operational improvement is most needed.

Business Objective

Turn patient, encounter, procedure, and cost data into actionable insights that support better care delivery, resource allocation, documentation, and financial decisions.

2.

Business Questions

Business Problem

Hospital leaders lack a single decision view connecting patient demand, clinical utilization, documentation quality, and financial burden. This makes it harder to identify cost drivers, prioritize resources, and target operational improvement.

Key Questions

Five questions guide the analysis and management discussion.

  • Patient Demand Which patient segments and encounter types drive hospital utilization?
  • Clinical Activity Which procedures, diagnoses, and service lines account for the greatest activity?
  • Cost Drivers Which encounter classes and procedures contribute most to healthcare costs?
  • Patient Burden How are healthcare costs shared between payers and patients?
  • Operational Gaps Where do documentation and resource-utilization issues create improvement opportunities?
Decision Objective Turn healthcare evidence into focused care, capacity, cost-management, and data-quality actions.
3.

Key Insights

What the dashboard evidence reveals about patient demand, clinical utilization, financial pressure, and data quality.

01

Financial burden is concentrated on patients

Observation

Patients carry $70.42M of the $101.5M total claim cost, while payer coverage is $31.10M.

Analysis

Approximately 69% of total claim cost is patient out-of-pocket, compared with 31% covered by payers.

Impact

High patient cost exposure may create affordability and access pressure.

Recommendation

Expand payer partnerships and patient financial-assistance pathways.

02

An aging population shapes hospital demand

Observation

645 of 974 patients are seniors, with an average patient age of 70.24 years.

Analysis

Older adults represent the dominant patient segment in the analyzed population.

Impact

Chronic-disease, geriatric, preventive, and follow-up demand is structurally important.

Recommendation

Strengthen preventive care, follow-up, chronic-care, and care-coordination programs.

03

Ambulatory care is the largest activity and cost pool

Observation

Ambulatory encounters total 12.5K and generate $36.3M in total claim cost.

Analysis

Ambulatory care leads both encounter volume and total cost among the encounter classes.

Impact

It is a major capacity, throughput, and resource-allocation lever.

Recommendation

Optimize ambulatory capacity and expand preventive-service pathways.

04

Clinical costs are concentrated in high-cost procedures

Observation

Electrical cardioversion contributes approximately $35.82M in procedure cost.

Analysis

It represents more than half of the $69.18M shown across the leading procedure-cost list.

Impact

A small number of high-cost procedures can materially influence clinical spend.

Recommendation

Review high-cost pathways, resource utilization, and length-of-stay patterns.

05

Clinical documentation is a major data-quality gap

Observation

19,541 of 27,891 encounters are recorded as “No diagnosis.”

Analysis

Approximately 70% of encounters lack a recorded diagnosis in the dashboard.

Impact

Incomplete documentation constrains clinical reporting, analytical insight, and reimbursement.

Recommendation

Strengthen physician documentation, coding accuracy, and Clinical Documentation Improvement.
