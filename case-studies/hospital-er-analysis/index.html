1.

Overview

Project Title

Hospital ER Analysis

Business Case

Emergency departments must balance patient demand, service capacity, waiting time, admissions, referrals, and patient experience. Without a consolidated view of these operational signals, it becomes difficult to identify pressure points and prioritize improvement.

Business Objective

Use ER visit, arrival, shift, admission, referral, wait-time, and satisfaction data to identify where operational efficiency and patient experience can be improved.

2.

Business Questions

Business Problem

Emergency department stakeholders need a consolidated view of patient demand, operational pressure, patient pathways, and patient experience to identify where performance gaps exist and prioritize improvement opportunities.

Key Questions

The analysis was designed to answer four key questions across emergency department demand, operations, patient pathways, and experience.

  • How much demand is the ER handling?
  • When does the ER experience the greatest operational pressure?
  • How are patients moving through the emergency department?
  • Where does patient experience require improvement?
3.

Key Insights

The analysis identified seven decision-focused insights across demand, operations, patient pathways, and patient experience.

1.

Demand is concentrated

Observation

9.22K ER visits were recorded; the highest daily volume reached 1,377 and the highest hourly volume reached 436 at 23:00.

Analysis

Arrival volume is uneven across time, creating identifiable demand peaks.

Impact

Peak periods can create avoidable capacity and congestion pressure.

Recommendation

Align staffing and support coverage with observed peak arrival windows.

2.

Referral demand is concentrated

Observation

General Practice generated 1,840 referrals, versus 995 from Orthopedics and fewer from the remaining departments.

Analysis

A small number of departments account for a large share of referral activity.

Impact

Improving a high-volume pathway can affect a meaningful portion of the referral workload.

Recommendation

Prioritize referral coordination and pathway review in high-volume departments.

3.

Afternoon shows a stronger operating pattern

Observation

Afternoon recorded the highest admission rate at 51.03% and the lowest average wait at 34.66 minutes.

Analysis

The shift combines comparatively strong throughput with the shortest wait.

Impact

Its operating practices may provide a useful internal benchmark.

Recommendation

Review staffing, workflow, and workload practices from the Afternoon shift for replication.

4.

Patient experience needs attention

Observation

Average satisfaction was 5.47/10; low ratings were 12.5% versus 7.2% high ratings.

Analysis

Negative experience signals are stronger than positive experience signals.

Impact

Patient experience represents a clear service-quality improvement opportunity.

Recommendation

Track wait time and satisfaction together and target service-recovery actions.

5.

Wait time and satisfaction move together

Observation

The 0–15 minute wait group averaged 5.70 satisfaction, compared with 5.38 for the 31–45 minute group.

Analysis

Shorter waits correspond with stronger satisfaction in the dashboard.

Impact

Reducing waiting-time friction may support better patient experience.

Recommendation

Use wait-time bands alongside satisfaction to identify service-recovery opportunities.

6.

Experience varies by referral pathway

Observation

Satisfaction ranged from 5.91 in Gastroenterology to 5.25 in Renal.

Analysis

There is a 0.66-point spread between the highest and lowest department averages shown.

Impact

Improvement effort can be targeted instead of applied uniformly.

Recommendation

Review lower-scoring pathways for process or service-experience friction.

7.

Positive experience is not yet dominant

Observation

High ratings of 8–10 were 7.2%, while low ratings of 0–4 were 12.5%.

Analysis

Low-rating responses exceed high-rating responses.

Impact

Service quality improvement should be treated as an active management priority.

Recommendation

Create a recurring satisfaction review tied to operational metrics.
